Propulsion Explorer — The Four Questions

1. What am I seeing?

An illustrative 3D model of an asymmetric electrostatic electrode assembly inspired by Exodus Propulsion Technologies patent US11511891B2 (FIGS. 4–8). It features an asymmetric high-voltage knife-edge blade cathode opposite a planar ground counter-electrode inside a grounded conductive enclosure.

2. Why does it happen?

Sharp conductor edges concentrate electric field lines (E ∝ 1/r). Maxwell electrostatic stress (Txx = ½ε0Ex2) exerts tension pulling on the blade tip. Conventional electric field vectors E = -∇Φ point from higher potential (0 V, ground) toward lower potential (-20 kV, cathode). By Newton's third law, opposing reaction forces act on the ground electrode, the dielectric potting, and surrounding chamber walls.

3. What changes it?

Applied voltage scales electrostatic force quadratically (F ∝ V2). Taper sharpness increases local peak field gradients. Dielectric permittivity alters polarization traction. Chamber wall proximity alters image-charge boundary coupling.

4. How could we test it?

To determine if net thrust exists, the entire isolated test article must be mounted on a calibrated micro-Newton torsion balance in high vacuum (< 10-5 Torr). Discriminating null controls must isolate corona ion wind, outgassing, electrostatic wall forces, and thermal convection (Tajmar & Schreiber 2020).
