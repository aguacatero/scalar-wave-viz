What am I seeing?

Propulsion Explorer — The Four Questions

1. What am I seeing?

An asymmetric high-voltage electrode assembly inspired by Exodus Propulsion Technologies patent US11511891B2 (FIGS. 4–8). A tapered knife-edge blade is charged to high voltage opposite a planar ground electrode inside an enclosure.

2. Why does it happen?

Sharp conductor edges concentrate electric field lines ($E \propto 1/r$). Maxwell stress exerts electrostatic tension ($T_{xx} = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E_x^2$) pulling on the blade tip. Simultaneously, opposing electrostatic forces pull on the ground electrode, the dielectric material, and the surrounding chamber walls.

3. What changes it?

Voltage scales all electrostatic forces quadratically ($F \propto V^2$). Taper sharpness increases peak field. Dielectric permittivity alters polarization traction. Chamber wall proximity alters image-charge return coupling.

4. How could we test it?

To determine if net thrust exists, the entire closed system must be isolated on a micro-Newton balance in high vacuum ($< 10^{-5}\text{ Torr}$). Null controls must eliminate ion wind, electrostatic wall interactions, and thermal buoyancy.
