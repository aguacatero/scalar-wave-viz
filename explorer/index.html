The Iceberg Learning Model

Physics Reference: Four Questions

1. What am I seeing? An illustrative 3D model of an asymmetric electrostatic electrode assembly inspired by Exodus patent US11511891B2 (FIGS. 4–8).

2. Why does it happen? Sharp conductor edges concentrate electric field lines (E ∝ 1/r). Maxwell electrostatic stress exerts tension pulling on the blade tip. In general 3D electrostatics, the vacuum stress tensor component is Txx = ε0(Ex2 − ½|E|2) = ½ε0(Ex2 − Ey2 − Ez2); the simplified formula ½ε0Ex2 is a 1D approximation strictly valid only where transverse fields vanish (Ey = Ez = 0). Conventional electric field vectors E = -∇Φ point from higher potential (0 V, ground) toward lower potential (-20 kV, cathode). By Newton's third law, opposing reaction forces act on the ground electrode, the dielectric potting, and surrounding chamber walls.

3. What changes it? Applied voltage scales electrostatic force quadratically (F ∝ V2). Taper sharpness increases local peak field gradients. Dielectric permittivity alters polarization traction. Chamber wall proximity alters image-charge boundary coupling.

4. How could we test it? Discriminating null controls in high vacuum: reverse electrode orientation, symmetric dummy electrodes, and solid shielding.
